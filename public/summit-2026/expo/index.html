    What AdaL does

    A San Francisco start-up building a video agent for taking your product to market. General-purpose agents got good at code. AdaL does the same for video.

    Give it a prompt and point it at your product: a changelog, a screen recording, a few screenshots. It plans the cut, then generates the voice-over, music, images and captions, and edits it all into a video. Launch videos, product demos, UGC ads, event recaps, customer stories and explainers.

  • CaseMate

    Growth stage · Drishti Binda

    A legal technology platform that fills the gap between generic AI tools and what it's actually like to go through a legal process. It's built for people representing themselves, and for the legal professionals who work with them.

    Pick where you are (separation, parenting, property, protection orders, visiting Australia) and CaseMate turns the official process into a path you can follow: what happens, which forms are involved, what they ask for, what they cost and what comes next. Forms are broken down field by field, legal terms are explained in plain English, and every step cites the official source. It's an information and navigation tool, not a legal service. The Western Australian Family Violence Restraining Order path is free.

    At the booth, try the live platform. Work through a real user journey: find the right court or tribunal, get to the official forms and resources, and see what the procedure needs and what happens next. The team will be there to talk about where AI fits in access to justice.

    The AI part: AI combined with structured, jurisdiction-specific procedural information, so answers come from the official process rather than a generic model's best guess. The aim is to make complex systems easier to navigate and cut down procedural errors. CaseMate doesn't use your information to train AI models.

  • fmsg.ai
    fmsg

    Early stage · Mark Mennell

    Messaging for the AI era. fmsg gives every person and every program its own address, so people and AI agents can message each other securely. Think email, minus the spam, the quoted thread histories and the vendor lock-in.

    At the booth, see an interactive demo: AI agents working together live over fmsg.ai, and you can join the conversation yourself. Humans are participants, not spectators.

    The AI part: agents get their own addresses, often as a sub-address of yours like @you_claude@fmsg.ai, and connect through a hosted MCP server, a web API or a CLI. Every message has a verified sender and is cryptographically linked so it can't be altered. Allow lists, rate limits and anomaly and loop detection control who can message whom, which matters once agents start talking to other agents.

    It's built on fmsg, an open standard anyone can host, with a published specification and an IETF Internet-Draft. Made in Australia, and the full stack can run on your own premises.

  • Keluvia

    Prize: to be revealed soon!

    Early stage · Muthu Murugesan

    A meeting tool for business analysts, product managers, project managers and consultants. It records the conversation in your browser, with no bot joining the call, and turns it into the outputs that kind of meeting should produce.

    At the booth you can:

    • Run a short mock requirements meeting with them and watch it turn into requirements, user stories, assumptions, open questions and a process map in a couple of minutes.
    • Ask questions of a past meeting ("what did we decide about X?") and get the exact quote, speaker and timestamp.
    • Try meeting prep: set a goal and get an agenda or focus areas to run the meeting with.
    • Bring a real project problem and work through it with them live.

    The AI part: the output changes with the type of meeting. A requirements session, a stakeholder review and a change impact meeting each give different results, not one generic summary.

    • Speech-to-text transcription with speaker labels.
    • Claude models that pull out requirements, decisions, risks and actions, and build BPMN process maps and customer journey maps from the conversation.
    • A project knowledge base using embeddings and retrieval (RAG), so you can ask questions across meetings and documents and get answers tied to a source.
    • AI-assisted meeting prep that works from the goal you set and the documents you upload.

    It's built for privacy. Audio is deleted after transcription, there's an Australian data residency option, and zero data retention with their AI providers is in progress.

    Prize: to be revealed soon!

  • Podyssey

    Prize: to be revealed soon!

    Early stage · Ahmad Saleem

    A search engine for podcasts. Podyssey scans millions of podcasts for conversations and mentions, and listeners can curate their own content from what it finds.

    Their new B2B platform is for marketing agencies, brands and PR teams. It transcribes podcasts as they publish, in 32 languages, and finds every time your brand or a competitor is named, pinned to the second with the audio behind it. Each mention is read for tone, so a host praising a rival counts for the rival. Alerts land in Slack, Teams, email or a webhook, and share of voice is weighted by chart position and reach.     At the booth, see how the platform works. They say there's nothing else like it out there.

    The AI part: the backend runs on a language model Podyssey built to process and ingest audio. Various other AI models then stitch the content together.

  • ROGR

    Early stage · Co-founder Tasveer Mann

    Compliance and booking software for mining and labour hire contractors. Before a worker can go to site they need drug and alcohol tests, medicals and tickets. Right now mobilisation officers track all of that by hand, across emails, spreadsheets and phone calls to clinics. ROGR shows who's ready for site, what's missing and what's about to expire, checked against each client's own site rules.

    At the booth, try the ROGR browser extension live: check a crew against site rules, see who's blocked and why, send booking links to workers and confirm their results. Their pilot partner, a WA crane hire contractor, is using it now.

    The AI part: ROGR reads messy schedule spreadsheets and worker documents in any format and pulls out the compliance data. It checks each worker against the client and site rules, applying whichever is strictest, and explains every flag in plain English. It also writes the messages to workers, labour agencies and clinics. A person approves everything before it goes out, so the mobilisation officer stays accountable and still saves hours every day.

    Built by co-founders Tasveer and Roshan Mann, with backing from Venture, the University of Western Australia's incubator.

  • Senstrali

    Early stage · Founder Jason Milne

    A Perth start-up building early warning for conveyor roller failure. Today someone walks the belt and listens for faults, which only catches damage once it's loud enough to hear from the walkway, and misses rollers that fail between walks. Senstrali Triage listens continuously with contact ultrasound and vibration sensors, grades each zone of the conveyor against its own baseline, and gives maintenance teams more confidence in the stop-or-run call.

    At the booth, see the physical hardware with live sensor demos, and a screen showing the AI side: agentic coding workflows, and electronics enclosures designed with AI and 3D printed.

    The AI part: Senstrali is a one-person start-up that leans heavily on AI to speed up the engineering. That covers firmware and software, sensor hardware design, signal processing and machine learning, as well as the website, brainstorming and marketing. Jason has spent 20 years designing sensors for extreme environments.

    At the booth, watch ShadowCell attack a deliberately vulnerable web app live and see how it proves a flaw is real. Then tell us what you're building, and we'll show you what an attacker would look at first.

Hosting and AI processing stay in Australia.

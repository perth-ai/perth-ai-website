Breaking AI: How to Hack Intelligent Systems
Abstract

What happens when someone deliberately tries to make an AI system behave in ways its developers never intended? AI applications introduce new attack surfaces that traditional software security practices don't fully address.

More

From adversarial examples that fool computer vision models to prompt injection attacks that manipulate LLMs and autonomous agents, intelligent systems can fail in surprising ways. Drawing on his background in adversarial machine learning and AI security research, Camilo demonstrates how these attacks work, why they succeed, and what developers can do to defend against them — including why relying exclusively on model guardrails is insufficient. Expect technical demonstrations, real attack scenarios and practical lessons for building more secure AI systems. For AI engineers, security professionals, software developers and technical leaders deploying AI applications.

Luca Intini

My 20 AI Teammates Never Sleep
Abstract

Nine months ago I stopped writing specs for other people's software and built a team that never sleeps: about 20 AI agents. One is the lead and takes my orders. The others each own a product: a remote-jobs board that reads thousands of ads a night, a back office chasing my tax deductions and medical refunds, a trading research lab. They write the code, deploy to AWS, watch the budget and email me a report before breakfast.

More

The trading lab is the busiest department. Small learner agents watch trading videos on YouTube and read papers, GitHub and Reddit, then turn the ideas worth testing into strategies. Bulat Almukhametov

AI in Physical Safety
Abstract

AI now writes code, reads X-rays and drafts legal contracts. But on a factory floor, a construction site or an LNG plant, the only question that matters is whether it can stop someone getting hurt. In 2025, 222 Australian workers didn't come home — the highest toll since 2012.

More

This lightning talk is a frontline safety professional's honest field report on AI in physical safety. Most EHS teams now use AI in some form, yet only a small fraction have it embedded in how safety actually runs. Bulat separates what is genuinely working — computer vision for PPE, vehicle and ergonomic risks — from what is still mostly slideware, and explains why data foundations, skills and a competent person signing off matter more than the model.

He then shares what Perth's SHEDAI community has been building and testing at its AI Safety Sandbox workshops: an AI investigation assistant, a local privacy firewall that strips personal data before anything reaches the cloud, and HSE chatbots built by AI and Safety professionals on Telegram and Microsoft Teams — all following one pattern: local first, privacy gate, cloud only for hard reasoning, human decides.

For decades, safety has been one of the most conservative professions there is, and for good reason. But conservative shouldn’t mean left behind. Maybe it’s time we led the change instead of waiting for it.

Bio

Bulat Almukhametov is an HSE professional and AI enablement practitioner based in Perth, helping safety professionals work with AI, not around it. He brings more than ten years in dedicated HSE roles and almost two decades across Oil & Gas, LNG, construction and manufacturing internationally.

More

Since 2022 he has been building the Australian chapter of his health and safety career at Holcim Australia in Perth. Outside his day job, Bulat builds practical AI prototypes for frontline safety and runs the first AI Safety Sandbox workshops in Perth, which are free in-person sessions for safety professionals. In 2024 he founded SHEDAI, an international community for safety professionals exploring Big data and AI, and mentors early-career practitioners. He holds an International NEBOSH and ISO certifications, an Advanced Diploma in Health, Safety & Risk Management from Oxford Brookes University, and a Bachelor's degree in Applied IT and Economics from USATU.
